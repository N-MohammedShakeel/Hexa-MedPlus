SECTION 1: SCOPE, OBJECTIVES, CLINICAL GOVERNANCE & TARGET POPULATION
================================================================================
1.1 Objective:
This clinical protocol establishes standardized, evidence-based guidelines for 
the diagnosis, glycemic control, lipid-lowering therapy, neuroimaging evaluation, 
and secondary prevention for adult patients presenting with Type 2 Diabetes 
Mellitus (T2DM) complicated by established or suspected Cerebrovascular Disease 
(CVD), Acute Ischemic Stroke (AIS), Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA), and mixed 
dyslipidemia.

1.2 Target Patient Population:
- Adult patients (age >= 18 years) with confirmed or newly diagnosed T2DM.
- Patients presenting with acute neurological deficits, focal motor weakness, 
  transient ischemic symptoms, or altered sensory perception.
- Diabetics with co-existing atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), 
  essential hypertension, and hypercholesterolemia.

1.3 Clinical Decision Support Integration:
All clinical pathways in this protocol are integrated with the Hexa MedPlus AI 
Decision Support System. Ingested protocol knowledge directly governs AI 
diagnostic recommendations, ICD-10 code extraction, and safety contraindication 
alerts during encounter documentation.

SECTION 2: INITIAL DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP, STAGING & RISK STRATIFICATION
================================================================================
2.1 Mandatory Diagnostic Battery upon Presentation:
Every patient evaluated under this protocol must receive the following core 
diagnostic battery within 2 hours of clinical admission or presentation:
  a. Venous Blood Glucose & Point-of-Care Capillary Glucose
  b. Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) automated high-performance liquid chromatography
  c. Fasting Lipid Panel: Total Cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, Triglycerides
  d. Comprehensive Metabolic Panel: Serum Creatinine, eGFR, Blood Urea Nitrogen 
     (BUN), Serum Sodium, Potassium, Chloride, Bicarbonate
  e. Urinalysis with Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio (uACR)
  f. 12-Lead Electrocardiogram (ECG) to screen for silent myocardial ischemia, 
     atrial fibrillation, and left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH).

2.2 Glycemic Diagnostic Thresholds (ADA / EASD Standard):
- Normal: Fasting Plasma Glucose < 100 mg/dL, HbA1c < 5.7%
- Prediabetes / Impaired Fasting Glucose: FPG 100-125 mg/dL, HbA1c 5.7% - 6.4%
- Diabetes Mellitus: FPG >= 126 mg/dL (confirmed on repeat), HbA1c >= 6.5%, 
  or Random Plasma Glucose >= 200 mg/dL with classic symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia).

2.3 Cardiovascular & Cerebrovascular Risk Categorization:
- VERY HIGH RISK: T2DM with established ASCVD (prior stroke, myocardial infarction, 
  peripheral arterial disease) or target organ damage (eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73m2, 
  uACR > 300 mg/g). Target LDL-C: < 55 mg/dL with >= 50% reduction from baseline.
- HIGH RISK: T2DM duration >= 10 years with at least one additional risk factor 
  (hypertension, smoking, dyslipidemia) without target organ damage. Target LDL-C: < 70 mg/dL.

SECTION 3: GLYCEMIC MANAGEMENT & PHARMACOTHERAPY DECISION TREES
================================================================================
3.1 Glycemic Targets:
- Standard Target: HbA1c < 7.0% (Fasting BG 80-130 mg/dL, Postprandial < 180 mg/dL).
- Relaxed Target (Frailty, Elderly, High Hypoglycemia Risk, History of Severe Stroke): 
  HbA1c < 7.5% - 8.0%.

3.2 First-Line Pharmacotherapy:
- Metformin Hydrochloride:
  * Initial dose: 500 mg orally once daily with meals, titrating weekly to 
    1000 mg BID (maximum 2000 mg/day).
  * Contraindications: eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2, acute metabolic acidosis, severe 
    hepatic impairment, or hemodynamic instability.
  * Hold Metformin 48 hours prior to and post iodinated radiocontrast administration.

3.3 Second-Line Combination Therapy (Cardio-Renal Protection Preference):
For patients with established ASCVD or high stroke risk, add:
- GLP-1 Receptor Agonist (GLP-1 RA) with proven cardiovascular benefit 
  (Semaglutide or Dulaglutide) to reduce secondary ischemic stroke and major adverse 
  cardiovascular events (MACE).
- SGLT-2 Inhibitor (Empagliflozin 10-25 mg daily or Dapagliflozin 10 mg daily) if 
  co-existing heart failure (HFrEF/HFpEF) or chronic kidney disease (uACR > 30 mg/g).

3.4 Basal Insulin Initiation Algorithm:
Initiate basal insulin (Glargine U-100 or Degludec) when HbA1c >= 9.0% or symptomatic 
hyperglycemia with catabolism is present. Start at 10 units subcutaneously once daily 
at bedtime (or 0.1-0.2 units/kg/day), titrating by 2 units every 3 days until fasting 
blood glucose is consistently within target range (80-130 mg/dL).

SECTION 4: LIPID MANAGEMENT & ATHEROSCLEROTIC CARDIOVASCULAR RISK REDUCTION
================================================================================
4.1 Statin Intensity Protocol:
All patients with T2DM aged 40-75 years with co-existing vascular disease require 
High-Intensity Statin Therapy regardless of baseline lipid levels:
  * Atorvastatin 40 mg to 80 mg orally daily, OR
  * Rosuvastatin 20 mg to 40 mg orally daily.
Goal: Achieve >= 50% reduction in baseline LDL-C and absolute LDL-C < 70 mg/dL 
(< 55 mg/dL for recurrent ischemic events).

4.2 Escalation Protocol for Refractory Hyperlipidemia:
- If LDL-C remains >= 70 mg/dL despite maximum tolerated high-intensity statin 
  after 6-12 weeks:
  * Step 1: Add Ezetimibe 10 mg orally daily.
  * Step 2: If LDL-C remains above target in very high-risk secondary stroke 
    prevention, add PCSK9 inhibitor (Evolocumab 140 mg SC every 2 weeks or 
    Alirocumab 75-150 mg SC every 2 weeks).

4.3 Hypertriglyceridemia Management:
- Fasting Triglycerides 150-499 mg/dL: Optimize glycemic control, implement lifestyle 
  dietary restriction, intensify statin therapy.
- Fasting Triglycerides >= 500 mg/dL: Initiate Fenofibrate 134-200 mg daily or 
  Icosapent Ethyl 2 g BID to mitigate acute pancreatitis risk. (Avoid Gemfibrozil 
  co-administration with statins due to rhabdomyolysis risk).

SECTION 5: ACUTE ISCHEMIC STROKE & TRANSIENT ISCHEMIC ATTACK (TIA) PATHWAYS
================================================================================
5.1 Hyperacute Triage Protocol (Time is Brain):
- Onset < 4.5 Hours: Immediate Code Stroke activation, urgent non-contrast CT Brain, 
  fingerstick glucose, and evaluation for intravenous thrombolysis (IV Alteplase 
  0.9 mg/kg or Tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg).
- Onset 4.5 - 24 Hours: CT Angiography (CTA) + CT Perfusion / MRI Diffusion-Weighted 
  Imaging (DWI) for mechanical thrombectomy screening in Large Vessel Occlusion (LVO).

5.2 In-Hospital Secondary Stroke Assessment:
For patients presenting beyond the acute thrombolysis window or admitted with subacute 
cerebral ischemia:
  * Neurological examination with NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS) scoring documented daily.
  * Continuous cardiac telemetry for minimum 48 hours to detect paroxysmal Atrial 
    Fibrillation (AFib).
  * Transthoracic Echocardiogram (TTE) with bubble study to evaluate cardioembolic 
    source (left ventricular thrombus, patent foramen ovale).
  * Carotid Duplex Ultrasound to quantify internal carotid artery stenosis.

5.3 Blood Pressure Management in Acute Ischemic Stroke:
- Non-thrombolysis candidates: Permissive hypertension allowed up to 220/120 mmHg 
  for first 48 hours to maintain cerebral collateral perfusion.
- Thrombolysis candidates: Blood pressure must be maintained strictly < 185/110 mmHg 
  prior to thrombolytic infusion, and < 180/105 mmHg for 24 hours post-infusion using 
  IV Labetalol or IV Nicardipine infusions.

SECTION 6: NEUROIMAGING PROTOCOLS (CT, MRI BRAIN, MRA & CAROTID DOPPLER)
================================================================================
6.1 MRI Brain Standard Stroke Protocol:
- Sequence 1: Diffusion-Weighted Imaging (DWI) & Apparent Diffusion Coefficient (ADC) 
  to detect acute cytotoxic edema and distinguish hyperacute ischemia from old infarcts.
- Sequence 2: T2-Weighted Fluid-Attenuated Inversion Recovery (FLAIR) to identify 
  subacute/chronic infarcts and periventricular white matter microvascular changes.
- Sequence 3: T1-Weighted Spin Echo (Pre- and Post-Contrast) for anatomical staging.
- Sequence 4: Gradient Recalled Echo (GRE) or Susceptibility-Weighted Imaging (SWI) 
  to screen for microhemorrhages, hemorrhagic transformation, and cavernous malformations.

6.2 Imaging Finding Interpretations & Action Triggers:
- Restricted Diffusion on DWI/ADC (bright on DWI, dark on ADC): Confirms acute 
  ischemic infarction. Initiate urgent antithrombotic therapy and neuro-monitoring.
- Chronic Leukoaraiosis / Periventricular T2 Hyperintensities: Indicates chronic small 
  vessel ischemic disease secondary to longstanding hypertension and microvascular diabetes.
- Hemorrhagic Transformation (HI-1, HI-2, PH-1, PH-2): Immediate cessation of all 
  antithrombotic, antiplatelet, and anticoagulant agents. Urgent repeat CT at 24 hours.

SECTION 7: ANTITHROMBOTIC & ANTICOAGULATION PROTOCOLS
================================================================================
7.1 Non-Cardioembolic Ischemic Stroke / High-Risk TIA:
- Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT):
  * Indication: Minor ischemic stroke (NIHSS <= 3) or high-risk TIA (ABCD2 score >= 4).
  * Regimen: Aspirin 81-100 mg daily + Clopidogrel 75 mg daily (with a 300 mg loading 
    dose of Clopidogrel on Day 1).
  * Duration: Continue DAPT for strictly 21 to 90 days, followed by long-term 
    single-agent antiplatelet monotherapy (Aspirin 81 mg daily or Clopidogrel 75 mg daily).

7.2 Cardioembolic Stroke (Atrial Fibrillation / LV Thrombus):
- Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs) preferred over Warfarin:
  * Apixaban 5 mg orally BID (reduce to 2.5 mg BID if >= 2 of: age >= 80, weight <= 60 kg, 
    serum creatinine >= 1.5 mg/dL).
  * Rivaroxaban 20 mg orally once daily with evening meal (15 mg daily if eGFR 15-49 mL/min).
  * Timing of DOAC initiation post-stroke: Day 1-3 for TIA, Day 3-6 for minor stroke, 
    Day 12-14 for moderate-to-severe stroke to prevent hemorrhagic conversion.

SECTION 8: LABORATORY MONITORING PROTOCOLS & RENAL SAFETY DOSE ADJUSTMENTS
================================================================================
8.1 Laboratory Surveillance Intervals:
- Inpatient: Fasting glucose daily; basic metabolic panel every 48 hours; HbA1c on admission.
- Post-Discharge:
  * HbA1c: Every 3 months until target achieved (< 7.0%), then every 6 months.
  * Lipid Panel: At 6-12 weeks post-statin adjustment, then annually.
  * Serum Creatinine & Potassium: At 2-4 weeks after initiating ACE-Inhibitor, ARB, 
    or SGLT-2 inhibitor.
  * Liver Function Tests (ALT/AST): Baseline prior to high-intensity statin, repeat 
    only if clinically symptomatic (jaundice, right upper quadrant pain, unexplained fatigue).

8.2 Renal Impairment Dosage Adjustment Table:
+-------------------+--------------------+---------------------------------------+
| Medication        | eGFR Threshold     | Action / Dose Adjustment               |
+-------------------+--------------------+---------------------------------------+
| Metformin         | eGFR 30-44 mL/min  | Max dose 1000 mg/day; monitor q3m     |

SECTION 9: INPATIENT GLYCEMIC MANAGEMENT & POST-ACUTE DISCHARGE PATHWAYS
================================================================================
9.1 Inpatient Ward Protocol:
- Target Inpatient Blood Glucose Range: 140 - 180 mg/dL (7.8 - 10.0 mmol/L).
- Discontinue oral antihyperglycemic agents (Metformin, SGLT-2i, Sulfonylureas) 
  during acute critical illness and unstable stroke phases.
- Utilize Subcutaneous Basal-Bolus Insulin regimen with pre-meal rapid-acting insulin 
  (Lispro or Aspart) and bedtime basal insulin (Glargine). Sliding-scale insulin alone 
  is strictly discouraged as monotherapy.

9.2 Multidisciplinary Post-Acute Discharge Checklist:
  [ ] Blood Pressure controlled: Target < 130/80 mmHg with ACE-Inhibitor / ARB.
  [ ] Lipid-lowering therapy confirmed: High-intensity statin prescribed.
  [ ] Antiplatelet / Anticoagulation plan documented with explicit duration.
  [ ] Diabetes management reconciled with patient and family education completed.
  [ ] Neuro-rehabilitation evaluation (Physical Therapy, Occupational Therapy, Speech).
  [ ] Carotid imaging results reviewed and vascular surgery referral placed if stenosis > 70%.
  [ ] Outpatient Endocrinology and Neurology follow-up scheduled within 14-21 days.
